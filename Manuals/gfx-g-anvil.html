Drum machines

G Anvil

A two-voice analogue percussion module - one big kick and one snare / metal voice - with a 16-step sequencer.

Overview

G Anvil is a dual-voice analogue percussion module in the spirit of the heavy-duty German drum modules that built industrial music and techno. There are no samples: both voices are synthesised as they play.

The KICK is a bridged-T style oscillator with an exponential pitch sweep, a click for the attack and a hard, lopsided drive. The SNARE / METAL voice uses two detuned square oscillators and white noise through a resonant multi-mode filter: with METAL at 0 it is a fat snare body, turned up it becomes an inharmonic clang with ring modulation. Each voice has its own drive, and BUS SAT can push the whole module into saturation.

A two-row, 16-step sequencer plays the two voices in time with REAPER's transport, and both voices also play from MIDI notes, with velocity.

Reach for it for

  • Big, distorted techno and industrial kicks
  • Long, booming dub and rumble kicks
  • Metallic clanks, rimshots and noisy snares from one voice
  • Gabber and hardcore kicks with the drive turned right up
  • Quick two-voice patterns right inside REAPER, or layering under another drum machine

Loading and playing it

  1. Insert G Anvil on a new track. It is an instrument: it makes its own sound and needs no audio input.
  2. Make sure PLAY (in TRANSPORT) is lit, then press play in REAPER. The sequencer follows the project's tempo and position.
  3. Click a voice's orange LED to hear it, choose a preset, and edit the steps in the grid.
  4. To play it from MIDI, arm the track with a MIDI input and record monitoring on, or put a MIDI item on the track (see MIDI notes below). Switch PLAY off if you want MIDI only. The MIDI light in the header flashes when a drum note arrives.

KICK

Click the LED at the top left to hear the kick.

PITCH
Where the kick's pitch lands after the sweep, 30 - 150 Hz (50 Hz by default).
SWEEP
How far above PITCH the sweep starts, 0 - 48 semitones (24 by default). 0 gives a flat, tonal boom; more gives a punchier drop.
S.TIME
How fast the pitch falls, 2 - 250 ms (28 ms by default). Short for a tight punch, long for a laser-like zap.
DECAY
How long the kick rings, 40 - 2500 ms (420 ms by default), measured to -60 dB.
WAVE
Morphs the oscillator from a pure sine (SINE) to a triangle (TRIANGLE), 0 - 100% (10% by default). More triangle adds harmonics and helps the kick cut through on small speakers.
CLICK
The level of the attack click, a short pulse and a burst of noise, 0 - 100% (35% by default).
C.TONE
The click's colour, 0 - 100% (50% by default): from a dark thud to a bright tick. Brighter clicks are also shorter.
DRIVE
A hard, lopsided analogue-style saturation, 0 - 100% (25% by default). Low settings thicken the kick; high settings flatten it into a square-edged distorted kick.
LEVEL
The kick's volume, 0 - 100% (85% by default).
PAN
Position, -100 (left) to +100 (right); CENTRE by default.

SNARE / METAL

Click the LED at the top left to hear the snare.

TUNE
The pitch of the two square oscillators, 80 - 1200 Hz (210 Hz by default).
METAL
0 - 100% (20% by default). Spreads the two oscillators apart and adds ring modulation: from a fat, nearly unison snare body to the classic inharmonic metal clang. It also opens up the tone's brightness.
T.DECAY
The decay of the tone, 10 - 2000 ms (110 ms by default).
N.DECAY
The decay of the noise, 10 - 2000 ms (190 ms by default).
NOISE
The balance of tone and noise, from ALL TONE (0%) to ALL NOISE (100%); 60% by default.
SNAP
The attack, 0 - 100% (50% by default): a quick level jump and a pitch blip at the start of each hit.
CUTOFF
The filter frequency, 100 Hz - 16 kHz (7.5 kHz by default).
RESO
The filter's resonance, 0 - 100% (20% by default). The level is kept steady as you turn it up.
LP / BP / HP (MODE)
The filter type: low-pass (the default, for a full snare), band-pass (a focused, boxy or ringing sound) or high-pass (thin, bright and clangy).
DRIVE
The same hard saturation as the kick, 0 - 100% (15% by default).
LEVEL
The snare's volume, 0 - 100% (75% by default).
PAN
Position, -100 (left) to +100 (right); CENTRE by default.

MASTER

LEVEL
Output level, -24 to +12 dB (0 dB by default).
BUS SAT
Lopsided saturation on the whole module, 0 - 100% (10% by default). It glues the two voices together and adds weight; high settings crunch hard.
L / R meters
The output level, left and right.

SEQUENCER

Two rows of 16 sixteenth-note steps, KICK on top and SNARE below. A step is a rest, a hit (orange) or an accent (red, with a light bar). The step that is playing is highlighted, and the LED next to each row's name flashes as the voice plays.

Steps
Click a step to set or clear it; drag to paint along the rows. Shift-click sets an accent; right-click switches a step between hit and accent.

The pattern is saved with the project and with each preset.

TRANSPORT

PLAY
The sequencer on or off (on by default). When on, it plays whenever REAPER's transport is playing, locked to the project tempo and position.
Display
The step and the tempo while playing. With PLAY on and the transport stopped it reads WAITING FOR PLAY; with PLAY off, SEQ OFF - MIDI ONLY.
SWING
Delays the off-beat sixteenths, 0 - 75% (0% by default).
ACC (ACCENT)
How much louder accents are than normal hits, 0 - 100% (50% by default). At 0 every step plays at full level; at 100 normal hits are 45% quieter than accents.
CLEAR
Clears both rows.

The bottom strip

DRIFT
0 - 100% (30% by default). Every hit is detuned a little at random, and the tunings, the snare filter and the click tone wander slowly, like real analogue circuits. At 0% every hit is identical.
MIDI (CH)
The MIDI channel: OMNI (all channels, the default) or 1 - 16.
STEREO / MONO
STEREO keeps the two voices where PAN puts them; MONO folds the output to mono.
SAT
Band saturation on the way out: only the lows and highs are saturated (the mids stay clean), with a soft curve that loosens as the level rises. 0% by default.
OUTPUT CLIP
A soft clipper on the output (always on) catches overs at the ceiling.

Knobs: drag up or down (Shift for fine moves); double-click or Ctrl-click to reset a knob to its default.

MIDI notes

G Anvil plays from these notes, with velocity: it sets the level, and softer hits are a little darker. Other MIDI passes straight through.

KICK
Notes 35 and 36 (C1)
SNARE
Notes 38 (D1), 39 and 40
RIM
Note 37: the snare voice, shorter and more tonal, with the filter opened up

Presets

Presets set both voices, BUS SAT, SWING, ACCENT and the pattern. MASTER LEVEL, DRIFT, PLAY, the MIDI channel, STEREO / MONO and the output stage are left as they are.

Init
The default kit: a clean, punchy kick on every beat and a snare on 2 and 4.
Industrial Thud
A longer, driven kick and a dark band-pass snare with lots of noise and a ringing filter.
Techno Rumble Kick
A long, low 1.4-second kick with an off-beat high-pass metal tick on the snare voice.
Classic Punch
A bright-clicking, tight kick and a clean snare, with a touch of swing.
Distorted Hardcore
A heavily driven kick with a deep sweep and a crunchy band-pass snare; BUS SAT at 40%.
Metal Clank
The snare voice at full METAL with a long tone: a clanging anvil hit, in a broken pattern.
Rimshot Tick
A short, clicky kick and a very short, high-pitched high-pass snare: a rimshot tick.
Deep Dub
A soft, low kick that rings for 1.8 seconds and a gentle, long snare, with swing.
Gabber
A fast-sweeping kick with full drive and lots of triangle, and BUS SAT at 50%.
Minimal Click
A short, clicky kick with almost no drive and a tiny high metal tick, with swing.
Warehouse Snare
A broken kick pattern with a big, noisy, driven band-pass snare.
EBM Stomp
A slow-sweeping driven kick with plenty of triangle and a thick low-pass snare.

Tips

  • For a long techno kick, set DECAY over 1 second, SWEEP low and S.TIME around 30 - 60 ms, then add DRIVE to taste.
  • The kick's DRIVE and BUS SAT add a lot of level: lower the voice LEVEL as you turn them up.
  • Turn METAL up and NOISE down, with the filter in BP or HP, for cowbells, clanks and bell-like hits.
  • Use note 37 from MIDI for rimshots between the snares without changing the snare settings.
  • Set DRIFT to 0 for a machine-tight loop; leave some in for a more alive, hardware feel.
