Instruments

G Mark 73

A lean tine electric piano: modelled tines and pickup, no samples, tremolo or auto-pan, up to 32 voices.

Overview

G Mark 73 is a tine electric piano in the spirit of the classic tiny freeware e-piano plug-ins: a handful of controls, a light touch on the CPU, and the sound of a 70s stage piano. Like everything in GFX it uses no samples. Each note is a struck tine, modelled with three decaying resonators: the tine itself, its tonebar, and the bright bell partial of the hammer strike. These are read by a magnetic pickup set slightly off-centre, so soft notes are round and pure and hard notes bark.

One LFO does two jobs, as on the original plug-in: turn MODULATION right for tremolo, left for auto-pan. WIDTH spreads the keyboard across the stereo field, low notes left and high notes right. The sustain pedal (CC64) works.

Reach for it for

  • Classic tine electric piano: soul, jazz, ballads
  • Suitcase tremolo and wide auto-pan pads
  • Barking, driven rock and funk keys
  • Bell-like, glassy high tines
  • A light e-piano when your session is already heavy

Controls

TINES

DECAY
How long notes ring when held: from about 0.8 s to 15 s at middle C. Low notes ring longer, high notes shorter, as on the real thing.
RELEASE
How quickly a note stops when you let go: the damper, from 40 ms to about 1.3 s.
HARDNESS
The hammer: more bell at the start of each note, more pickup bark on hard hits, and a brighter tone overall. Low settings give a soft, felt-like sound.
TREBLE
A high shelf at 2.5 kHz, -6 to +12 dB.

MODULATION

MODULATION
Turn right for tremolo (the level pulses), left for auto-pan (the sound moves between the speakers). The centre is off. The light blinks with the LFO.
RATE
The LFO's speed, 0.1 to 10 Hz.

PLAYING

VELOCITY
How much key velocity changes the level (hardness always follows velocity).
WIDTH
How far the keys spread across the stereo field: 0% is mono.
VOICES
Polyphony, 8 to 32. When all voices are busy the oldest note is taken, released notes first.

TUNING

FINE
Fine tuning, -50 to +50 cents.
RANDOM
A random detune on every note, up to 50 cents (4 by default): a little life, or an out-of-tune upright.

MASTER

DRIFT
See Drift below.
DRIVE
Overdrive: drives the pickup and the output for a broken-up, growling tone.
VOLUME
-24 to +6 dB.
SAT
The GFX band saturation on the output (0% by default).
OFF / CLIP / LIM
The GFX output stage. CLIP (the default) is a soft clipper just under 0 dB; LIM is a look-ahead limiter. The band frequencies, bias and ceiling are in the parameter list.
Meters
Output level, left and right.

The header

PRESETS
Opens the patch list (or click the display). An asterisk after the patch number means you have changed it.
◄ / ►
Step through the patches.
MIDI
Blinks on each note.
S / M / L
Panel size: small, medium or large. The panel also scales to fit the plugin window.
?
Opens a pop-up with the design credit, the GFX website address and the address of this manual. Click anywhere to close it.

Drift

DRIFT (0 - 100%, 50% by default) is the universal GFX instrument drift: the pitch and the tone wander slowly, and every new note lands slightly off tune. 50% is the standard amount, 100% twice that, 0 steady; the wander also speeds up as you turn it up.

Tips

  • Play with dynamics: HARDNESS and the pickup respond to velocity, so the tone changes as much as the level.
  • For classic tremolo, try MODULATION around +50% with RATE between 4 and 6 Hz.
  • Raise VOICES only if you hear notes cut off in long sustained passages; fewer voices use less CPU.
  • For an old, characterful instrument, turn RANDOM and DRIFT up together.
