Instruments

G Pocket 70

A late-90s sound module: 90 synthesised patches, from pianos to pads to a drum kit, with effects built in.

Overview

G Pocket 70 is a sound module in the spirit of the late-90s desktop boxes: pick a patch and play. There are 90 patches in ten groups - synthetic pianos, FM and chorus electric pianos, clav, drawbar organ, bells and glass, guitars and plucks, strings, pads, choirs, brass, leads, basses and a drum kit on the GM drum map. Every sound is synthesised; there are no samples.

Each patch comes with its own effects: the GFX Mod Rack (tremolo, chorus, vibe and delay) and a reverb (room, hall or plate). The EDIT page lets you move a patch from where it stands - brighter, darker, faster or slower envelopes - and choosing a patch puts everything back.

It plays up to 16 notes at once. GFX drift is built in, fixed and subtle: the tuning wanders a little and every note lands slightly differently.

Reach for it for

  • Quick, playable sounds for sketching and demos
  • 90s-style keys, pads and choirs
  • Basses and leads without building a patch
  • A GM-style drum kit for programming beats
  • Layering a second keyboard part with built-in effects

Playing it

  1. Insert G Pocket 70 on a track as an instrument (it has no audio input; it plays from MIDI).
  2. Arm the track for recording with a MIDI input and turn on record monitoring, or draw notes in a MIDI item.
  3. Click a patch name on the patch list to choose it. The MIDI IN light blinks green when notes arrive.
  4. The mod wheel (CC1) adds vibrato, the sustain pedal (CC64) holds notes, pitch bend follows BEND, and CC120 / CC123 stop all notes. A program change 0 - 89 on the MIDI channel selects patches 1 - 90.
  5. If you use more than one MIDI channel, set MIDI CH on the EDIT page to the one you want; OMNI plays on all.

Controls

The top bar

Display
Shows the patch's group, number and name. While you turn a knob on the EDIT page it shows that knob's name and value for a moment instead. Click the display to go to the patch list.
< / >
Step to the previous or next patch (wrapping round at the ends).
EDIT
Opens the EDIT page.
MIDI IN
Blinks green when a note arrives.
VOLUME
Output level, -24 to +6 dB.
S / M / L
Panel size: small, medium or large (it opens at large). The panel also scales to fit the plugin window.
?
Opens a pop-up with the design credit, the GFX website address and the address of this manual. Click anywhere to close it.

The patch list

Every patch, by group, in six columns. Click a name to choose it; the selected patch is lit blue.

EDIT page

The TONE, FILTER ENVELOPE and AMP ENVELOPE knobs move the patch from where it stands: the centre (or 100%) is the patch as made, and choosing a patch puts them back. Controls that the current patch has no use for are dimmed.

TONE

TONE
Brighter or darker, by up to 1.5 octaves either way from the centre.
FILTER
Moves the patch's filter, from -6 to +3 octaves. On the FM, plucked-string, metal-bar, piano and organ patches (which have no filter of their own) it works a low-pass on the output, so only turning it down has an effect.
RESONANCE
Adds resonance to the filter, 0 to 100%.

FILTER ENVELOPE

Works on the analogue and GM-style patches. Session Piano, Bright Piano, Chorus EP, FM E-Piano, Clav, Glockenspiel, Drawbar Organ and Drum Kit have no filter envelope: the row is dimmed and reads (not on this patch). On those patches the AMP ENVELOPE's ATTACK and SUSTAIN are dimmed too, DECAY is dimmed on the organ and the kit, and RELEASE on the kit.

AMOUNT
How far the envelope moves the filter, 0 to 200% of the patch's own amount.
ATTACK / DECAY / RELEASE
Scale the patch's filter envelope times, from 10% (faster) to 400% (slower).
SUSTAIN
Moves the patch's sustain level, -100 to +100.

AMP ENVELOPE

ATTACK / DECAY / RELEASE
Scale the patch's volume envelope times, from 10% (faster) to 400% (slower). On struck and plucked sounds DECAY sets how long they ring.
SUSTAIN
Moves the patch's sustain level, -100 to +100.

PLAY

VELOCITY
0 to 100% (70% by default): how much playing harder makes notes louder (and on many patches brighter). 0 ignores velocity.
TUNE
Fine tuning, -50 to +50 cents.
BEND
Pitch-bend range, 0 to 12 semitones (2 by default).
MIDI CH
The MIDI channel it listens to: OMNI (all) or 1 to 16. Click it for the list.

REVERB

ROOM / HALL / PLATE
The reverb type: a small room, a large hall, or a dense, bright plate.
PRE
Pre-delay before the reverb starts, 0 to 150 ms.
DECAY
Reverb time, 0.2 to 10 s.
DAMP
How quickly the high frequencies die away in the tail, 0 to 100%.
MIX
How much reverb is added, 0 to 100%.

MOD RACK

Four effects working side by side on the instrument: TREMOLO, CHORUS, VIBE and DELAY. Each adds its own effect to the dry sound. They are off when their depth or mix is at zero.

SYNC
Click to step through off, SYNC (straight), SYNC D (dotted) and SYNC T (triplet). With sync on, RATE (or TIME on the delay) becomes a note value from 4 BARS to 1/32, locked to the project tempo.
L / C / R
Which outputs the module works on: L the left side, R the right side, C a mono centre feed to both. With C and a side on together, the sides are offset for stereo movement.
TREMOLO: RATE, DEPTH
Volume wobble: 0.1 to 15 Hz, depth 0 to 100%.
CHORUS: RATE, DEPTH, MIX
Chorus: 0.05 to 8 Hz, depth 0 to 100%, mix 0 to 100%.
VIBE: RATE, DEPTH
A four-stage phase-shift swirl: 0.1 to 12 Hz, depth 0 to 100%.
DELAY: TIME, FDBK, MIX
Echo: 20 to 1500 ms (or a synced note value), feedback 0 to 95%, mix 0 to 100%.

OUTPUT

SAT
Band saturation on the way out: only the lows and highs are saturated (the mids stay clean), with a soft curve that loosens as the level rises.
OFF / CLIP / LIMIT
The final stage: nothing, a soft clipper (no latency, the default), or a lookahead limiter (3 ms latency, compensated by REAPER).
MONO BELOW
Makes everything below this frequency mono, from off (20 Hz) up to 500 Hz.
WIDTH
Widens the stereo image above that, 0 to 100% (up to twice the side signal).

Tips

  • Choosing a patch resets the EDIT knobs and loads its own reverb and Mod Rack, so make your edits after you have picked the patch.
  • Native Dance follows the project tempo: its loop locks to the timeline when the project plays.
  • Turn on the delay's SYNC so the echoes sit in time with the track.
  • On bass patches, set MONO BELOW to around 120 Hz to keep the low end centred if you add stereo chorus or width.
