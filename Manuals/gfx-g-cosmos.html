G Cosmos

An 8-voice analogue pad synthesizer in the spirit of the classic 80s polys, with a detuned layer, chorus and an ambient delay.

Overview

G Cosmos is a polyphonic pad synth in the spirit of the classic 80s polys, with a nod to the layered shimmer of the late-80s digital synths. One oscillator per voice gives saw, pulse (with PWM), a sub-octave and noise; a second, detuned saw (the LAYER) can sit at the same pitch or an octave either side for width and shimmer.

The voices go through a 24 dB filter with its own envelope, a fixed high-pass with four settings, and an amp envelope. The famous bucket-brigade stereo chorus (I, II or both) and an ambient stereo delay with diffused, gently moving repeats finish the sound.

DRIFT makes it behave like real analogue hardware: the tuning and filter wander for the whole instrument and for each voice on its own, and every note lands slightly differently in tune.

Reach for it for

  • Lush, slow-moving pads and drones
  • Warm 80s poly chords and string machines
  • PWM and choir-like textures
  • Filter-sweep pads for builds and intros
  • Ambient beds with long, diffused delay tails

Playing it

G Cosmos is an instrument: it has no audio input and makes its sound from MIDI.

  1. Insert G Cosmos on a track (as the first plug-in, or after a MIDI effect).
  2. Arm the track for recording and set its input to your MIDI keyboard (or draw notes in a MIDI item).
  3. Pick a patch with < and >, or click the patch display for the list.
  4. Play. The MIDI light flashes on each note, and the display shows how many of the 8 voices are sounding.
  5. If you use a multi-channel MIDI setup, set CH in the PERFORMANCE section to the channel you want; OMNI listens to all of them.

G Cosmos responds to pitch bend (+/-2 semitones), the mod wheel (CC1 adds vibrato through the LFO, up to 40 cents), the sustain pedal (CC64) and all-notes-off (CC120 and CC123).

Controls

The header

Patch display
Shows the patch number and name, how many of the 8 voices are playing and the chorus setting. Click it to open the patch list.
< and >
Step to the previous or next patch. A patch sets every sound control and VELOCITY; DRIFT, VOLUME and the MIDI channel are left as they are. Loading a patch stops any notes still sounding.
MIDI
Lights when a note arrives.
S / M / L
Panel size: small, medium or large. The panel also scales to fit the plugin window.
?
Opens a pop-up with the design credit, the GFX website address and the address of this manual. Click anywhere to close it.

LFO

RATE
LFO speed, 0.05 - 20 Hz.
DELAY
How long the LFO takes to fade in, 0 - 5 s. It starts again from the first key of each new phrase (when no other keys are held), so held chords grow vibrato and movement as they sustain.
SHAPE
SINE, TRI, SQUARE or RANDOM (stepped).
PITCH
Vibrato depth, 0 - 100 cents. The mod wheel adds up to 40 cents more.

The LFO also moves the filter (LFO in the VCF section) and the pulse width (PWM in the DCO section).

DCO

SAW
Level of the sawtooth, 0 - 100%.
PULSE
Level of the pulse wave, 0 - 100%.
PW
The pulse width, 5 - 95%. 50% is a square; narrower settings sound thinner and more nasal.
PWM
How much the LFO sweeps the pulse width, for the classic moving, chorused pulse sound.
SUB
Level of a square wave one octave below.
NOISE
Level of white noise, for breath and air.

LAYER

LEVEL
Level of the second, detuned saw, 0 - 100%.
DETUNE
How far the layer is detuned, 0 - 50 cents. Small amounts give a slow beating width; large amounts a thick, wide shimmer.
OCTAVE
The layer's octave: -1, 0 or +1.

HPF

0 / 1 / 2 / 3
The fixed high-pass after the filter. 0 BOOST lifts the bass below about 120 Hz; 1 FLAT leaves it alone; 2 and 3 THIN cut the lows (from about 240 Hz and 720 Hz), so the pad sits above a bass part.

VCF

CUTOFF
The 24 dB low-pass filter's cutoff, 20 Hz - 18 kHz.
RES
Resonance, 0 - 100%. High settings ring and sharpen the sweep.
ENV
How far the FILTER ENV moves the cutoff, -100 to +100%. Negative values sweep it down instead of up.
LFO
How much the LFO moves the cutoff, 0 - 100%.
KEY
Key tracking, 0 - 100%: how much higher notes open the filter. At 100% the cutoff follows the keyboard.

FILTER ENV and AMP ENV

Two separate ADSR envelopes: FILTER ENV moves the cutoff (by the ENV amount), AMP ENV shapes the volume.

ATTACK
Time to rise, 1 ms - 8 s.
DECAY
Time to fall to the sustain level, 5 ms - 10 s.
SUSTAIN
The level held while the key is down, 0 - 100%.
RELEASE
Time to die away after the key is let go, 5 ms - 15 s.

CHORUS

I / II
The bucket-brigade stereo chorus. I is a slow, gentle sweep; II is faster and deeper. Light both for I+II, where the two sweeps run together. With both off the chorus is out.

DELAY

An ambient stereo delay: the repeats bounce between left and right (the right side at three-quarters of the time), are diffused, move gently and get darker as they fade.

TIME
Delay time, 40 - 1500 ms.
FEEDBACK
How many repeats, 0 - 95%.
TONE
How dark the repeats are, 500 Hz - 12 kHz.
MIX
How loud the delay is, 0 - 100%.

PERFORMANCE

VELOCITY
How much playing harder makes notes louder, 0 - 100%. At 0% every note plays at the same level.
DRIFT
See Drift below.
VOLUME
Output level, -24 to +6 dB.
CH
The MIDI channel G Cosmos listens to: OMNI (all) or CH 1 to CH 16.

The output is softly clipped at -0.3 dBFS, so peaks never go over.

Drift

DRIFT
0 - 100% (25% by default). The tuning wanders slowly for the whole instrument (up to 20 cents at 100%) and for each voice on its own (up to 6 cents); the filter cutoff wanders the same way, for the whole instrument and per voice; the layer's detune drifts a little; and every note starts slightly out of tune (up to 6 cents). At 0% everything stays exact.

Tips

  • For pads that bloom, set the LFO DELAY to 1 - 2 s and add a little PITCH and filter LFO: each new chord starts still and grows movement.
  • In a busy mix, use HPF 2 or 3 so the pad stays out of the way of the bass.
  • A negative ENV with a high CUTOFF gives pads that darken as they sustain.
  • Chorus I+II with a little layer DETUNE is the widest setting; try it on a single held note before adding more voices.
  • Use VELOCITY at 0% for even sequenced pads, and higher for played parts.
