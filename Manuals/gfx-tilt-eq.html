EQ & filters

Tilt EQ

A one-knob tilt equaliser with high- and low-pass filters and mid / side tilt.

Overview

Tilt EQ is in the spirit of the classic one-knob hi-fi and mastering tilt controls. One knob seesaws the whole spectrum around a pivot point: turn it right and the top rises as the bottom falls (brighter), turn it left for darker. It is the quickest way to change the overall balance of a track or a mix without thinking about bands.

High- and low-pass filters with selectable slopes clean up the extremes, and the tilt can work on the whole stereo signal, on the middle only or on the sides only. A response window shows the combined curve.

Reach for it for

  • Quick brightness or warmth changes on a mix bus or master
  • Matching the tonal balance of different takes or stems
  • Brightening the centre or darkening the sides (MID / SIDE)
  • Steep high- and low-pass clean-up
  • Telephone and band-limited effects

Controls

Response window

Shows the combined curve of the filters, the tilt and OUTPUT from 20 Hz to 20 kHz, on a +/- 8 dB scale. The small triangle at the bottom marks the PIVOT.

High-pass

FREQ
The high-pass cutoff, 10 - 1000 Hz. Fully left = OFF.
Slope
The knob beside it: 6, 12 (default), 18, 24, 36 or 48 dB/oct. All slopes are Butterworth (maximally flat).

Tilt

TILT
The big knob. Right is BRIGHTER, left is DARKER; the word under it shows which, or FLAT at the centre. The value is the lift or cut at each end, up to 6 dB (12 dB end to end); hover over the knob to read it.
PIVOT
The balance point, 100 Hz - 5 kHz (800 Hz by default). Frequencies here stay where they are; the tilt rises on one side and falls on the other.
GENTLE / STEEP
GENTLE (default): a long, even 6 dB/oct slope across the whole range. STEEP: turns over more quickly around the pivot and leaves the far ends flatter.

Low-pass

FREQ
The low-pass cutoff, 1 - 22 kHz. Fully right = OFF.
Slope
The knob beside it: 6, 12 (default), 18, 24, 36 or 48 dB/oct, Butterworth.

Bottom row

STEREO / MID / SIDE
Where the tilt works: on both sides (STEREO, default), on the middle only or on the sides only - for example darken the sides and brighten the centre. The filters always work on both sides.
TRIM
Input level before anything else, from -24 to +12 dB.
GUARD
Click the TRIM knob to switch it; its LED sits at the knob's upper right. On by default. Gently eases very hot sources down towards about -18 dBFS average and catches fast peaks above -6 dBFS, so loud sources never overdrive the plugin. It never boosts quiet sources. The LED glows brighter while it is working.
SAT
Band saturation on the way out: only the lows and highs are saturated (the mids stay clean), with a soft curve that loosens as the level rises.
OFF / CLIP / LIMIT
The final stage: nothing, a soft clipper at -0.3 dBFS (no latency), or a lookahead limiter (3 ms latency, compensated by REAPER). CLIP is on by default and flashes red when it clips.
OUTPUT
Output level, -12 to +12 dB.
EQ IN
Switches the filters and tilt in or out (in by default). OUTPUT and the IN / OUT stages keep working.

The header

HORIZON / CLASSIC
Switches between the two looks: HORIZON (sky and sea, gold) and CLASSIC (cream and red). The sound is the same.
PRESETS
Opens the list of factory presets; the ◄ / ► keys step through them and the display shows the one in use (click it for the list too). Choosing one sets the tilt, filters, STEREO / MID / SIDE, OUTPUT and EQ IN; the input and output stages are left as they are. The last entry resets everything to the defaults.
S / M / L
Panel size: small, medium or large. The panel also scales to fit the plugin window.
?
Opens a pop-up with the design credit, the GFX website address and the address of this manual. Click anywhere to close it.

In REAPER's parameter list only

Bias / Sat low band / Sat high band / Ceiling
The rest of the output stage: the saturation's even-harmonic bias, where its low and high bands start (40 - 400 Hz and 2 - 16 kHz) and the clip ceiling or limiter threshold.

Tips

  • Small moves go a long way: 1 - 2 dB of tilt is often enough on a full mix.
  • Move the PIVOT to where the music's important energy sits; that part stays put while the balance shifts around it.
  • Use EQ IN to compare, and adjust OUTPUT so the louder version doesn't fool your ears.
  • SIDE with a darker tilt keeps wide cymbals and reverbs from getting harsh, without dulling the vocal in the centre.
