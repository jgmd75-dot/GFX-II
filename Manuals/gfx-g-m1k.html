G M1K

A six-voice analogue rack synth with 100 patches, a full editor and a built-in effects rack.

Overview

G M1K is a six-voice analogue synth module in the spirit of the late-80s matrix-modulation rack synths: a single rack unit full of patches, with a full editor behind the EDIT key. Each voice has two DCOs, a 24 dB low-pass VCF, two envelopes and two LFOs.

It comes with 100 factory patches in seven banks - KEYBOARD, STRINGS, WIND+PERC, SYNTH, BASS, LEAD and EFFECTS. Every patch also carries its own Mod Rack setting: chorus, tremolo, vibe and delay built into the unit.

It is an instrument: it has no audio input and plays from MIDI. It responds to pitch bend, the mod wheel (CC1), channel aftertouch, the sustain pedal (CC64) and all-notes-off (CC120 / CC123).

Reach for it for

  • Classic late-80s analogue pads, strings and brass
  • Punchy synth basses and mono leads with glide
  • Quick patch browsing - 100 sounds a click away
  • Building your own patches in the editor
  • Wide stereo sounds with the built-in Mod Rack

Loading and playing it

  1. Insert G M1K on a track (it is an instrument, so it needs no audio on the track).
  2. Arm the track for recording and set its input to your MIDI keyboard (or draw MIDI notes into an item on the track).
  3. Click the patch name window and choose a patch, or step through them with the < and > keys. The MIDI light flashes when notes arrive.
  4. If you play on a particular MIDI channel, set CH in the PLAY strip; OMNI listens to all 16.

Controls

The rack unit

Patch number
The red three-digit display shows the patch number, 000 to 099.
Patch name window
Shows the bank and the patch name. While you turn a control it shows that control's name and value for a moment instead. Click it for every patch, listed by bank.
< / >
Step to the previous or next patch (wrapping round from 099 to 000).
EDIT
Switches the lower half of the panel between the patch list and the editor.
BLACK / CREAM
Swaps the faceplate between the black early boxes and the cream 90s boxes. Looks only - the sound does not change.
MIDI
Lights when a note arrives.
VOLUME
The overall level, -24 to +6 dB.

The patch list

With EDIT off, the lower half lists all 100 patches under their bank headings. Click a name to load it; the current patch is highlighted.

DCO 1

WAVE
Switches the saw / triangle wave on or off.
PULSE
Switches the pulse wave on or off. WAVE and PULSE can both be on at once.
FREQ
Tuning in semitones, -24 to +24.
SHAPE
Morphs the wave from SAW (fully left) to TRI (fully right).
PW
Pulse width, 5 to 95% (50% is a square).

DCO 2

WAVE / PULSE / SHAPE / PW
As on DCO 1.
NOISE
Replaces DCO 2's waves with white noise.
SYNC
Hard-syncs DCO 2 to DCO 1. Tune DCO 2 up with FREQ (or sweep it with the pitch envelope) for the classic tearing sync sound.
FREQ
Tuning in semitones, -24 to +24.
DETUNE
Fine tuning against DCO 1, -50 to +50 cents (+7 by default) - the beating that makes two oscillators sound thick.

MIX

1 / 2
The balance between the two DCOs, from DCO 1 only to DCO 2 only.

VCF

A 24 dB per octave low-pass filter.

CUTOFF
The filter frequency, 20 Hz to 18 kHz.
RES
Resonance: a peak at the cutoff, for a sharper, more vocal or squelchy tone.
ENV 1
How far ENV 1 moves the cutoff, -100 to +100% (up to six octaves either way). Negative values close the filter as the envelope rises.
KEY
Keyboard tracking: how much the cutoff follows the notes you play (at 100% it moves one octave per octave), so high notes stay as bright as low ones.
FM
DCO 1 sweeps the cutoff at audio rate, for a clangorous, metallic edge.
VEL
Velocity opens the filter: harder notes are brighter (up to +5 octaves).

ENV 1 - VCF and ENV 2 - VCA

Two envelopes with the same controls. ENV 1 moves the filter (and, if you like, the pitch); ENV 2 shapes the volume of each note. The attack rises in a straight line; the decay and release fall away naturally.

DLY
A delay before the envelope starts, 0 to 3 s.
ATK
Attack time, 0.5 ms to 8 s.
DEC
Decay time to the sustain level, 5 ms to 10 s.
SUS
The level held while the key is down, 0 to 100%.
REL
Release time after the key is let go, 5 ms to 10 s.
PITCH (ENV 1 only)
ENV 1 bends the pitch, -24 to +24 semitones - for sync sweeps, zaps and drum-like drops.
DCO 2 / BOTH
Click to choose whether PITCH moves DCO 2 only or both DCOs.

LFO 1 - VIBRATO

SPEED
0.05 to 30 Hz.
DEPTH
Vibrato depth, 0 to 100 cents.
DELAY
The vibrato fades in over this time after each note, 0 to 5 s.
Wave box
Click to choose the shape: TRIANGLE, SAW UP, SAW DOWN, SQUARE, RANDOM (stepped) or NOISE (smooth random).

The mod wheel and channel aftertouch add more vibrato through LFO 1 (up to 50 cents), whichever is higher.

LFO 2

SPEED
0.05 to 30 Hz.
DEPTH
How much LFO 2 moves its destination, 0 to 100%.
Wave box
The shape, as on LFO 1.
TO PW / TO CUT / TO PITCH
The destination: the pulse width of both DCOs (PWM), the filter cutoff (up to +/-5 octaves) or the pitch (up to +/-12 semitones).

The Mod Rack

Four effects in parallel on the whole voice, saved with each patch. Each module has a SYNC key (click to step through off, SYNC straight, SYNC D dotted and SYNC T triplet): when synced, RATE or TIME becomes a note value from 4 BARS to 1/32, locked to the project tempo. The L, C and R keys pick which outputs the module feeds: left, a mono centre, and right. With C plus L or R, the outputs are offset from one another for a wider spread.

TREMOLO
RATE (0.1 - 15 Hz), DEPTH, and CHOP, which squares the wave off for a choppier, on-off tremolo.
CHORUS
RATE (0.05 - 8 Hz), DEPTH, MIX, and STRETCH, which lengthens the chorus delay and lowers its clock for a darker, grainier, warbling chorus.
VIBE
RATE (0.1 - 12 Hz), DEPTH, and LAMP, which makes the sweep lopsided and adds the throb of the old lamp-driven units.
DELAY
TIME (20 - 1500 ms), FDBK (0 - 95%), MIX, and RUNAWAY, which adds tape-like wow and flutter to the repeats and pushes the feedback harder.

PLAY strip

POLY / MONO / UNI
POLY: six voices. MONO: one voice, last-note priority and legato - a note played while another is held glides to the new pitch without restarting the envelopes, and letting go returns to the note still held. UNI (unison): all six voices on one note, detuned and spread across the stereo field, for a huge mono sound.
GLIDE
Portamento time, 0 to 1 s: the pitch slides from the previous note.
VELOCITY
How much velocity changes the volume, 0 to 100% (50% by default).
BEND
Pitch-bend range, 0 to 12 semitones.
DRIFT
See Drift below.
LEVEL
The patch level, -12 to +6 dB, saved with each patch to even out loud and quiet patches.
CH
MIDI channel: OMNI or 1 to 16.
SAT
Band saturation on the way out: only the lows and highs are saturated (the mids stay clean), with a soft curve that loosens as the level rises.
OFF / CLIP / LIMIT
The final stage: nothing, a soft clipper (no latency, the default), or a lookahead limiter (3 ms latency, compensated by REAPER), both holding peaks at -0.3 dBFS.
S / M / L
Panel size: small, medium or large. The panel also scales to fit the plugin window.
?
Opens a pop-up with the design credit, the GFX website address and the address of this manual. Click anywhere to close it.

Drift

DRIFT (0 - 100%, 25% by default) makes the module behave like real analogue hardware: the overall tuning wanders, each voice's two DCOs drift apart in fine pitch and pulse width, DCO 2 drifts against DCO 1, the filter breathes, every note lands slightly differently in tune, and the Mod Rack's free-running rates waver a little. At 0 it is perfectly steady.

Patches

Choosing a patch sets every DCO, VCF, envelope, LFO and Mod Rack setting, plus the voice mode (POLY / MONO / UNISON), GLIDE and the patch LEVEL. VOLUME, VELOCITY, BEND, DRIFT and the MIDI channel are left as they are.

Tips

  • Your edits stay until you choose another patch. To keep an edited patch, save it as a REAPER preset (the + button at the top of the plugin window).
  • Use LEVEL rather than VOLUME to balance one patch against another - LEVEL is saved with the patch.
  • For a thick lead, try a MONO patch and switch to UNI: all six voices stack on one note.
  • Raise DETUNE on DCO 2 and add the CHORUS module for wide, classic pads.
  • If a patch's echo clashes with your song, switch the DELAY module's SYNC on so its time follows the project tempo.
