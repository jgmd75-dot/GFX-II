E-Verb

Four European broadcast reverbs of the 1980s in one, each with its own faceplate.

Overview

E-Verb recreates four styles of European broadcast reverb from the 80s, built for acoustic precision rather than lushness: there is no chorusing in the tail, and the input runs through a transformer. The MODEL keys change both the algorithm and the faceplate.

ROOM SIM works out the early reflections from the shape and size of a real room, then feeds an unmodulated network that holds up in mono. BROADCAST is a compact, warm plate and hall. DIALOGUE is a dense, quick-recovering reverb tuned for speech. NON-LIN is a gated / reverse processor.

Every parameter sits on its own seven-segment display, with the reverb time set in seconds and adjusted separately at the low and high end, and the room size given in cubic metres.

Reach for it for

  • Natural rooms for voices, acoustic instruments and drums
  • Speech and voice-over that needs space without blur
  • Warm plates and halls with a vintage converter sound
  • Gated and reverse effects on drums
  • Mono-compatible reverb

Controls

Using the displays

Every parameter has its own display. Click a display to hand it to the data wheel (its frame lights up); drag the display up or down (hold Shift for fine steps), use the mouse wheel over it, or press its - and + keys (hold them to repeat). Double-click a display to return it to its default.

Meters

IN / REV
Two LED columns in 3 dB steps: the input level and the reverb's output level.

MODEL

ROOM SIM
A room simulator: the reflections are worked out from the room's shape (first and second order, with a slight difference between the ears), then an 8-line network with no modulation at all - true room modes that hold up in mono.
BROADCAST
The compact broadcast plate / hall: damped parallel combs into allpasses, a warm loop and a heavier input transformer.
DIALOGUE
A dense allpass tank tuned for speech: high diffusion, fast recovery, no flutter.
NON-LIN
A long tapped line with a shaped envelope instead of a natural decay. TIME sets the length of the shape (up to about 1.25 s, stretched further by SIZE).
SHAPE (RM / GT / RV)
NON-LIN only. RM: a falling, room-like envelope. GT: gate - flat, then cut off. RV: reverse - the sound rises, then stops.
VINTAGE
Runs the reverb through the converters of the era, with the clock rate and bit depth of each model (14-bit on BROADCAST, 16-bit on the others; 31.25 to 40 kHz).

1st REFLECTION

DELAY
When the early reflections start, 0 to 120 ms.
LEVEL
The level of the early reflections, -30 to +6 dB; fully down reads OFF.

REVERB

DELAY
The pre-delay before the reverb tail, 0 to 250 ms.
LEVEL
The level of the tail, -30 to +6 dB; fully down reads OFF.

REVERB TIME

HIGH END
A multiplier for the decay time of the highs, 0.1 to 1.5 x. Below 1 the top dies away faster, like real air absorption.
TIME
The decay time at mid frequencies, 0.1 to 20 seconds.
LOW END
A multiplier for the decay time of the lows (below about 250 Hz), 0.5 to 2.5 x.

ROOM

SIZE
The room volume, 10 to 12000 cubic metres. It sets the spacing of the reflections and the size of the reverb network (and the length of the NON-LIN shape).

REFLECTIONS

DENSITY
How many early reflections are used, 0 to 100%. Low settings give a few distinct reflections; high settings a dense cluster.
DIFFUSION
How much the input is smeared before it enters the reverb, 0 to 100%. More diffusion gives a smoother, less grainy onset.

FILTER

LO CUT
A high-pass on the reverb, 20 to 1000 Hz.
HI CUT
A low-pass on the reverb, 1 to 20 kHz (shown in kHz).

CHARACTER

DRIVE
The input transformer, 0 to 100%: the lows saturate first, for weight and warmth. BROADCAST's transformer is heavier.
WIDTH
The stereo width of the reverb, 0 to 150%. 0 is mono; above 100 is wider than natural.
DRIFT
0 to 100%. A slow wander of the air absorption (the high-end damping) - never the delay lengths, so there is no pitch movement. On NON-LIN the tap levels also shift slightly. The wander speeds up as DRIFT goes up.

OUTPUT

MIX
Dry / reverb balance, 0 to 100% (30% by default).

PARAMETERS

Data wheel
Changes whichever display was clicked last (named under SELECTED). Drag it (Shift for fine steps) or use the mouse wheel.
Program display
Shows the preset in use (P1 to P10), or P-- when none is loaded.
LIST
Opens the list of all presets by name. The last entry resets everything to the defaults.
◄ / ►
Step to the previous or next preset.
1 - 9, 0
Memory keys: recall presets 1 to 9, and 0 for preset 10.

IN / OUT

TRIM
Input level before anything else, -24 to +12 dB.
GUARD
Off by default. Gently eases very hot sources down towards about -18 dBFS average and catches fast peaks above -6 dBFS. It never boosts quiet sources.
SAT
Band saturation on the way out, 0 to 100%: only the lows and highs are saturated (the mids stay clean), with a soft curve that loosens as the level rises.
LEVEL
Output level, -24 to +12 dB.

The header

Model name
The line beside the name changes with the model: DIGITAL ROOM SIMULATOR, DIGITAL BROADCAST REVERBERATOR, CONSOLE REVERBERATION UNIT or DIGITAL REVERBERATOR / PROCESSOR.
S / M / L
Panel size: small, medium or large. The panel also scales to fit the plugin window.
?
Opens a pop-up with the design credit, the GFX website address and the address of this manual. Click anywhere to close it.

Tips

  • For a natural room, start in ROOM SIM, set SIZE to the space you imagine, then adjust TIME; it holds up well when the mix is checked in mono.
  • Pull HIGH END below 1 to make a long reverb sound darker and further away without touching HI CUT.
  • On speech, DIALOGUE with a short TIME and LO CUT around 150 - 200 Hz adds space without smearing the words.
  • For the 80s gated drum sound, use NON-LIN with GT and set TIME to choose how long the gate stays open.
